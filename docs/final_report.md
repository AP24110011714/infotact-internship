## 1. Introduction

Hospitals record each step of a patient's care, but delays and systemic patterns stay hidden
in raw tables. CareFlow uses data analytics to explore patient admission records, clinical
measurements, and sepsis outcomes to surface meaningful patterns.

## 4. Clinical Results

### 4.1 Vital Signs by Sepsis Status

| Measurement | Sepsis-Positive Mean | Sepsis-Negative Mean |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature (°C) | **38.62** | 37.00 |
| Systolic BP (mmHg) | **100.51** | 118.08 |
| Diastolic BP (mmHg) | **62.89** | 74.86 |
| Heart Rate (bpm) | **112.76** | 77.94 |
| WBC Count (10⁹/L) | **14.94** | 7.47 |
| Lactate (mmol/L) | **3.81** | 1.19 |

> Sepsis-positive patients consistently show higher temperature, heart rate, WBC count,
> and lactate, and lower blood pressure — consistent with known sepsis physiology.

### 4.2 Sepsis Rate by Ward

| Ward | Patients | Sepsis+ | Sepsis Rate |

## 7. Recommendations

See `docs/recommendations.md`. Key recommendations:
1. Enrich dataset with timestamps per activity to enable full process mining (DFG, variants, conformance).
2. Add outcome columns (ICU length-of-stay, mortality) to train predictive models.
3. Apply SOFA or qSOFA scoring to validate sepsis flag.
4. Investigate ICU-A's 35.9% sepsis rate — may indicate patient acuity or referral patterns.

---

## 8. Limitations

- Dataset is **synthetic** and does not represent real patients or a real hospital.
- No event-sequence timestamps, so process mining (waiting time, rework, conformance) is not applicable to this dataset directly.
- Sepsis flag is derived from ward-stratified random distributions; it is not validated by clinical criteria.

---

## 9. Conclusion

CareFlow demonstrates a complete data analytics pipeline — from synthetic data generation
through cleaning, analysis, and interactive visualization — on a clinically realistic 5,000-patient
sepsis dataset. The tool successfully identifies ward-stratified sepsis patterns and vital-sign
differences that mirror known clinical sepsis markers.
